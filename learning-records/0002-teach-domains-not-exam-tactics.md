# Curriculum corrected: teach the five domains, not exam-answering tactics

The first curriculum opened with a lesson on how the examiners reason — distractor taxonomy,
heuristics for eliminating options. Kumar rejected it directly: *"You should teach my topics
which are asked in the exam, the 5 topics which are official... instead you are teaching me what
is the correct answer. This is the wrong approach."*

He was right, and the correction is not cosmetic. Exam-answering strategy is a way of
**extracting marks from knowledge you already hold**; it cannot substitute for the knowledge, and
teaching it first inverts the dependency. It also fails the mission's actual goal — the
credential is meant to certify architectural judgement he can use on Logiciel client work, and
distractor-spotting transfers to neither.

**Standing instruction for all future sessions:** lessons are organised by the five official
domains and their 30 task statements. Teach mechanism first (real field names, real enum values,
runnable code, primary source linked), then the tradeoff the mechanism creates, then the
judgement the exam tests. Any exam-technique material is confined to a short note before the
final mock, never a lesson.

[[0001-prior-knowledge-and-exam-target]] remains valid — the profile and timeline are unchanged.

**Evidence:** explicit, unambiguous user feedback with a stated reason. No quiz evidence yet;
this records a teaching-approach correction, not a knowledge state.
