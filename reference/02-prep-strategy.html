The single most repeated piece of advice

Tradeoffs, not features

Every source converges on this: candidates who succeed spend less time memorising features and more time understanding tradeoffs — when to enforce versus persuade, when to batch versus call synchronously, when to escalate versus resolve. The exam never asks "what does -p do" in isolation; it asks which of four architectures survives a stated production constraint.

The corollary: you still need the mechanics. You cannot evaluate "should this use the Batch API" without knowing it has no latency SLA and cannot do multi-turn tool calling. Mechanics are necessary; they are just not sufficient.

Anthropic's own recommendations (Exam Guide §7–8)

The guide names seven preparation activities and four hands-on exercises. These carry more weight than any third-party advice, because the same authors wrote the items.

Realistic adaptation for a 2–3 week plan

Building all four exercises properly is a multi-week project on its own. Prioritise Exercise 1 (agentic loop with escalation) and Exercise 2 (Claude Code team config): the first covers the heaviest domain and the second is quick given your daily Claude Code use. Read exercises 3 and 4 as designs rather than building them, unless the Domain 4 and 5 quizzes go badly.

Verify before you trust

Several widely-shared community write-ups state the domains incorrectly — merging Prompt Engineering with Context Management, or using an obsolete four-domain framing. One of the two study guides already on your machine (certification-preparation/claude-certified-architect/) has this problem. When a third-party source and the Exam Guide disagree, the Exam Guide is right.

Exam-day mechanics worth rehearsing

  • 120 minutes, 60 items = 2 minutes each. Scenario text is shared across a group of questions, so budget more for the first item under each scenario and less thereafter.
  • 4 scenarios drawn from 6. You cannot predict which; prepare all six contexts.
  • Read the qualifier. "First step", "most effective", "select two" all change the answer. Multiple-response items state how many to pick.
  • Closed book, proctored. Clear desk, no phone, no second monitor, government photo ID matching your registration exactly.
  • Free reschedule up to 24 hours before. Inside 24 hours you forfeit the fee.
  • Domain percentages on your score report are informational. Only the total scaled score decides pass/fail — so do not sacrifice a strong domain to shore up a weak one.
